=== start | Getting started
The Exam Generator keeps an approved question bank and builds inspection exams from it. Each page in the sidebar is one part of that work.

# Signing in
Your Windows sign-in finds you in the shared Personnel file, so most days you don't sign in at all. The sidebar shows only the pages your Exam Generator roles use: **My reviews** for SMEs, **Approvals** for the Department Head, XO and Senior Member, and the **Admin** pages for the admin. If you have no role yet, the app says so and names the admin to ask.

# Getting help
Click **Help** in the sidebar, or press **F1**, to open help for the page you are on. Choose any other topic from the list on the left, or type in the search box to find the topics that mention a word. Press **Enter** in the search box to open the first match.

# Go anywhere with Ctrl+K
Press **Ctrl+K** and start typing the name of a page. Press **Enter** to go to the first match, or use the **Up** and **Down** arrows to choose another. **Esc** closes it.

# Other things in the sidebar
- **My settings** holds your notice switches, your colors (**Appearance**) and **Sign out**.
- The **arrow** at the top folds the sidebar to icons, to give the page more room. Click it again to open it. Point at an icon to see its name.

=== dash | Dashboard
What waits on you, and the topics short of approved questions, on one page.

# The tiles
- **Reviews open**: the exam topics you're an SME of that nobody has signed off yet.
- **Approvals waiting**: the questions and exams at your step in the chain. Your own questions aren't counted, since you can't approve them, and neither are exams where you signed off a topic.
- **Sent back to you**: your questions an approver returned, to fix and resubmit.
- **Topics short**: the active topics with fewer approved questions than their templates need under the overlap rule. The alert under the tiles names them.

# Waiting on you
Everything above in one list, the longest waiting first. Click a row and **Open**, or double-click it, to go there: a review opens on **My reviews**, a question or exam on **Approvals**, and a question sent back in the editor.

# Recent activity
The last 20 things done in the app, newest first: saves, submissions, approvals, exams generated, and who did them. An admin sees everything. Everyone else sees what was done to the questions, exams and references they worked on themselves, to the questions they wrote, and to the exams they review.

# Turning the list off
Turn off **Dashboard list** under **My settings** to show the three queue tiles as **Off** and hide the list. The short topics and the recent activity still show.

=== review | My reviews
Each exam topic you review. When the department head generates an exam, every SME of each of its topics gets a review of that topic here. Any one of them checks the questions drawn for it, swaps any out and signs off. One sign-off completes the topic for all its SMEs.

# Your reviews
- **Open** shows the topics waiting for a sign-off, the oldest first. **Done** shows the ones you signed off, and the ones another SME signed off (**Closed**).
- Type in the **search box** to find a review by who the exam is for, its template or its topic. Use the funnels on the column captions to narrow the list, and click a caption to sort by it.

# Checking the questions
Pick a review to see it on the right: the exam it is on, the questions drawn for the topic, and the picked question in full under them, with its parts, answer options and references. A question marked **Given lately** was on one of the exams generated before this one (as many as the admin set for the overlap rule); one marked **Swapped** was put in by an SME, and **Swapped, given lately** is both. One marked **Replaced** is no longer the approved version in use: a newer version was approved, or the question was retired, after the exam was drawn. Swap it before you sign off: **Sign off** refuses while the topic has one, and **Swap** lists the newer version when there is one, so you can put it in its place. If a question is replaced after the topic was signed off, the topic comes back to its SMEs when the exam's last topic is signed off, so the exam never reaches the DH with an old version.

# Swapping and signing off
- **Swap** puts another approved question of the topic in place of the picked one. Double-click a question, or right-click it and pick **Swap**, to do the same. See **Swapping a question**.
- **Sign off** says the questions are right. The topic is then complete: its questions are settled for this exam, your review moves to **Done** and the other SMEs' reviews of it close. The app asks first, because it can't be undone.
- When the exam's last topic is signed off, the exam goes to the **DH** for approval.

# Sent back
An approver can send an exam back: one topic or all of them. Each topic sent back loses its sign-off, and you get a new open review of it here. The note on the right starts with who sent it back and why. Check the questions again, swap what the reason asks for, and sign off. When every topic is signed off again, the exam starts again at the **DH**.

Who may swap and sign off is checked again when you click: you must still be an SME of the topic with an open review of it. If another SME swapped a question on the topic or signed it off since you opened it, your swap or sign-off doesn't go through: the app says so and shows the topic as it stands now, so you only sign off the questions you have seen.

=== review.swap | Swapping a question
**Swap** opens a window listing the approved questions of the topic that aren't on the exam, those given lately last, and the newer version of the question you are swapping out, when it has one. Type in the search box to find one by its words or its number (Q12). Pick one to read it in full.

The note under it says whether the overlap rule allows it. At most the admin's share (30% unless changed) of a topic's questions may have been on the exams generated before this one; a swap that would take the topic over that is refused, so pick a question that wasn't given lately, or swap out one that was. A topic already over the limit (the DH agreed to repeats when the exam was generated) can swap one question given lately for another, but can't take in more. The exams generated after this one count it in their own window, so a question one of them holds can't come in when that would take that exam's topic over the rule. **Swap** puts the picked question on the exam. The exam's history records who swapped which question for which; **History** on the **Exams** page shows it.

=== approvals | Approvals
The questions waiting for your approval. Each approver has their own queue: a submitted question goes to the **DH**, then the **XO**, then the **SM**, and shows here while it waits at a step whose role you hold. The longest waiting is at the top. The **Exams** tab does the same for exams; see **Approving an exam**.

# Checking a question
Pick a row to see it on the right:
- The steps across the top show where it is: **SME** (written and submitted), **DH**, **XO**, **SM**. The ticked steps have approved it.
- Who wrote it, and who submitted it and when.
- The whole question as submitted: its text, points and portion marking, each part, the answer options with the correct one marked, the reference answers and the references it cites.
- **History** shows every version and who acted on it, with any reasons given.
- Search by words in the question, its topic or its number (Q12), and use the funnels on the column captions to narrow the list. Click a caption to sort by it.

# Approve or reject
- **Approve** sends it to the next step. At the **SM**'s step it goes into the bank: it is approved and can be drawn for exams. A new version replaces the version in use at that moment, which is retired (the app asks first). Exams that already have the old version keep it.
- **Reject** sends it back to its author with your reason, which is required. See **Rejecting a question**.
- Right-click a row for the same actions, and to show it in the question bank.

You can't approve or reject a question you wrote: another holder of the same role has to. Who may approve is checked again when you click, so a role taken away since you signed in counts at once. If someone else acted on the question first, the app says so and the list shows where it stands now.

=== approvals.reject | Rejecting a question
**Reject** opens a window that shows who the question goes back to and asks for the reason. The reason is required: say what to change.

The question becomes **Rejected** and leaves every approver's queue. Its author sees the reason in the question editor and in the question's **History**. The author, or any SME on its topic, changes it and submits it again, and it starts again at the **DH**. A rejected new version can't be started again from the approved version; change the rejected one instead.

=== approvals.exams | Approving an exam
When the SMEs have signed off every topic of an exam, it goes to the **DH**, then the **XO**, then the **SM**, like a question. It shows on the **Exams** tab while it waits at a step whose role you hold.

# Checking an exam
Pick a row to see it on the right: where it is in the chain, who it is for, who generated it, and its highest portion marking. Under that is the whole exam: each topic with who signed it off, then every question in order with its parts, options and **the answers**, which the Word document leaves out. A question replaced or retired since the exam was drawn is flagged there, and the final approval says so before it goes ahead. **History** shows who generated it, swapped its questions, signed off, approved and sent it back, and each time its Word document was made.

# Approve or send back
- **Approve** sends it to the next step. The **SM**'s approval is final: the app asks first, then stamps the exam's overall marking (its highest portion marking) and makes its Word document. If Word can't make it, the exam stays approved, and **Download a copy** on the **Exams** page makes it later.
- **Reject** opens a window: pick one topic or **(Every topic)**, check who gets it back, and give the reason, which is required. Those topics lose their sign-off and their SMEs get new reviews under **My reviews**. The exam's version goes up by one (E12 v2), and once every topic is signed off again it starts again at the **DH**. A topic with no active SME can't be sent back until the admin adds one; the window names it and keeps **Send back** greyed out.

You can't approve or send back an exam if you signed off one of its topics: another holder of the same role has to, so it doesn't show on your list. The DH who generated it may approve it, since its questions were drawn at random and checked by the SMEs.

=== exams | Exams
Every exam and where it stands, the newest first. The department head starts a new exam here with **New exam**; see **Generating an exam**.

# Finding an exam
- The chips keep one status: **SME review** (its topics are being checked), **In review** (waiting on an approver; **Waits on** says who), **Rejected** (sent back to its SMEs) and **Approved**.
- Type in the **search box** to find an exam by who it is for, its template or its number (E12). Use the funnels on the column captions to narrow the list, and click a caption to sort by it.

# The exam on the right
Pick a row to see it: where it is in the chain, who it is for and who generated it, and where it stands. A sent-back exam shows who sent it back and why; an approved one shows when and its overall marking. Under that, its topics: how many are signed off, and who signed off each.
- **Download a copy** saves the approved exam's Word document; see **Downloading an exam**.
- **History** shows who generated it, swapped its questions, signed off, approved, sent back and downloaded it, and each time its Word document was made, and when.
- Right-click a row for the same actions, and to open it in **Approvals** when it waits on you.

=== exams.download | Downloading an exam
Only an approved exam has a document. **Download a copy** asks where to save it and saves a copy of the Word document made at the **SM**'s approval. The app keeps the original in an **Exam documents** folder beside the data file; if it is missing, or the admin has reordered the classification levels since it was made, Download makes it again, which needs Word on this computer. Every download is recorded in the exam's **History**.

The document has a cover page (who it is for, the template, the exam's number and version, the date approved, and how many questions and points), then every question in order with its parts and options, but no answers. Each question and part starts with its portion marking, such as (C); its answer options count at the same marking. Each page is marked at the top and bottom with the highest marking on that page, the cover with the exam's overall marking, and the footer numbers the pages. If Word can't split the pages cleanly, every page carries the overall marking instead, and the app says so. If the order of the classification levels was changed after the exam was approved, every page carries the higher of the overall marking approved then and the exam's highest marking now, and the app says so.

=== exams.history | An exam's history
Everything that happened to one exam, newest first: who generated it, swapped its questions, signed off its topics, approved it, sent it back (with the reason), downloaded it, and made its Word document (with the marking it carries), each with when, at which approval step, and its version then. The rows are never changed or deleted.

Click **Copy** to put the history on the clipboard as text, to paste into an email or a report.

=== exams.new | Generating an exam
Only the department head (**DH**) generates exams. **New exam** opens a window:
- Pick a **template**. The topics it draws from show under it: how many questions each gets, how many approved questions it has, and how many of those weren't on the last exams. Topics turned off are left out.
- Type who the exam is for: the boat, schoolhouse or other command.
- **Generate** draws each topic's questions at random from the approved questions in use, and gives every SME of the topic a review of it under **My reviews**.

# The overlap rule
At most the admin's share (30% unless changed) of each topic's questions may have been on the last exams (10 unless changed), counted over every exam generated, whatever its template or who it was for. A new version of a question given lately counts as given. The draw takes questions not given lately first.

# When a topic can't be drawn
- **No SME**: no active SME reviews the topic. The admin adds one on the **Topics** page.
- **Short**: the topic has fewer approved questions than the template gives it.
- **Repeats**: the topic can only be filled with more questions from recent exams than the rule allows. **Generate** asks before it does that.

**Generate** can't be used while a topic is **No SME** or **Short**.

=== questions | Question bank
Every question in the bank, and the references they cite. Everyone can look; SMEs write and change questions.

# Finding a question
- Type in the **search box** to find questions by their words, their topic or their number (Q12).
- The **chips** show questions by status. **All** shows every version that isn't retired: each approved question, and a new version of it while that is being written or approved; **Retired** shows the versions a newer version replaced.
- The **funnel** on a column header shows only the rows with the values you tick. The chips' counts follow the search and the funnels.
- **Export** saves the rows shown as a CSV file or an Excel workbook.

# The question on the right
Pick a row to see the question: its status and who last acted on it, its text, topic, type, difficulty, points, portion marking, parts and references. **History** shows every version and who wrote, changed, submitted, approved or rejected it.

# Writing and changing questions (SMEs)
- SMEs write questions in the topics the admin made them an SME of (the **Topics** page). A question can be changed by its author or by any SME on its topic; anyone else opens it read-only.
- **New question** opens the editor on a blank question.
- **Edit**, or a double-click on the row, opens the question. A draft or a rejected question can be changed; one in review, approved or retired opens read-only.
- To change an approved question, use **More** > **Edit as new version**. The new version goes through the whole approval again.
- **More** > **Delete draft** deletes a draft that was never submitted and is on no exam.

# Statuses
- **Draft**: being written. Only drafts and rejected questions can be changed.
- **In Review**: submitted, waiting on the DH, then the XO, then the senior member.
- **Approved**: can be drawn for exams.
- **Rejected**: sent back with the approver's reason (shown in the editor and in History). Change it and submit it again.
- **Retired**: replaced by a newer version once that version was approved. It is kept for the record and never drawn.

# References
The **References** tab lists the documents questions cite, with how many current questions cite each. SMEs and the admin can:
- Correct a reference's title, number or revision and click **Save**. Every question citing it shows the change, so a different document should be a new reference instead.
- Click **New reference** to add one to the list.
- Use **More** to turn a reference off (it can't be cited again; questions citing it keep it), or to delete one that no question cites.
- Click **Show in the question list** to see only the questions citing it. **Show all questions** goes back to all of them.

Each addition, correction, turn off or on and deletion is kept in the audit record with who made it and when; a correction keeps the old title, number and revision too.

=== questions.history | A question's history
Every version of one question, newest first: who wrote, changed, submitted, approved, rejected and retired it, when, at which approval step, and any comments. The rows are never changed or deleted. A draft that was deleted no longer shows here, but its rows stay in the audit record.

Click **Copy** to put the history on the clipboard as text, to paste into an email or a report.

=== qedit | Question editor
Write a question with its parts, portion markings, answer options and references. Nothing reaches the bank until you click **Save**, so no one else sees a half-written question.

# The question's own fields
- **Topic**: type part of its name and pick it from the list. The list shows the topics you are an SME of.
- **Type**: multiple choice, true/false or short answer. Only multiple choice questions have answer options.
- **Difficulty** and **Points**. Points go in whole or half points, from 0.5 to 100.
- **Portion marking**: the question's classification marking. Once a question has parts, it is set from them: the highest part's marking.

# The tabs
- **Question**: the text as it will print. Extra spaces and blank lines are tidied when you save.
- **Parts**: a question with parts (A, B...) needs at least two. Each part has its own text, points, portion marking and reference answer, and the parts' points must add up to the question's. Parts are lettered by their order; the arrows move a part.
- **Answer options**: the choices of a multiple choice question, lettered A, B... in their order. With parts, each part has its own options: pick the part in the box beside the buttons before you click **Add option**. Select an option and click **Mark correct**; each question or part has one correct option.
- **References**: the documents the answer comes from, with the paragraph. **Cite a reference** picks one from the shared list or adds a new one.
- **Reference answer**: the answer key for the command. It is never printed on the exam.
- **History**: every version's audit rows.

# Saving and submitting
- **Save** (Ctrl+S) checks the question and keeps it as a draft. If something is missing, it says what and takes you to it.
- **Submit for approval** saves it, checks that it has references and, for multiple choice, at least two options with one correct in each group, then sends it to the department head. You can't change it while it is in review.
- If someone else saved the question while you had it open, Save says who and asks before saving over their changes.
- Leaving with unsaved changes asks whether to save them.

# Versions
An approved question is changed by starting a new version (**More** > **Edit as new version**). The approved version stays in use, and can still be drawn for exams, while the new version is written and goes through approval. When the new version gets its final approval it takes over and the old one is retired. A question can have only one new version open at a time. Deleting the new version's draft before it is submitted leaves the approved version as it was.

# More
- **Undo changes** drops your changes since the last save.
- **New question** starts a blank one.
- **Delete draft** deletes a draft that was never submitted and is on no exam.

=== qedit.part | A question's part
A part (A, B...) of a multipart question.
- **Label**: set by the part's place. Move the part to change it.
- **Points**: whole or half points. The line under the fields says what the question's points leave for this part.
- **Portion marking**: the part's own classification marking. The question takes the highest of its parts'.
- **Text**: the part as it will print.
- **Reference answer**: the part's answer key, never printed.

Click **OK** to keep the part in the question. It is saved to the bank with the question, when you click **Save** in the editor.

=== qedit.reference | Citing a reference
- **Cite one already listed**: type part of its title or number and pick it from the list. Only references turned on are listed.
- **Add a new reference**: give its title, number and revision as the document shows them. OK adds it to the shared list at once, so other SMEs can cite it too, even if you don't save the question.
- **Paragraph**: where in the reference the answer is, such as 3.2.1 or Table 4. It is optional.

To cite a different reference, remove the citation and cite the other one.

=== stats | Statistics
How many questions each topic gave on approved exams over a period, and how much of each topic came from one reference. The figures are per topic, and per reference within a topic.

# What counts
Only exams the Senior Member gave final approval to, by the day they did. A question given on three exams counts three times. Pick **6 months**, **12 months** or **Dates** for your own period, and a template to see one series of exams, or every template.

# Weak reference coverage
A topic where one reference gives 80% or more of the questions given (with 5 questions or more) has weak coverage: the exams test that one document again and again. The alert names these topics. Add questions from other references to them.

# The tables
- **Topics**: each topic's questions given, the exams it was on, how many references its questions cite, and the reference cited most with its share.
- **References**: click a topic to see every reference its questions cite, the most cited first. A question citing two references counts for both, so the shares can add up to more than 100%. A topic whose questions cite no reference shows a dash under **Top share**.

**Print** and **Export** take the topics table.

=== topics | Admin: Topics
Topics are the subjects questions are written in. Each has a number of questions it starts with on a template, and the SMEs who review its questions and exams.

# The list
Each row shows the topic, its SMEs, how many **approved** questions it has, and its status:
- **Active**: in use, with enough approved questions.
- **Short**: in use, but with fewer approved questions than the overlap rule needs for the most questions any active template gives it. New exams may not be able to keep to the rule for it.
- **Off**: turned off. Templates can't add it.

Type in the search box to find topics by name or description. Click a column caption to sort by it.

# Adding and changing a topic
Click **New topic** (or press **Ctrl+N**) and type its name. It starts active, with the questions per topic set under **Rules and lists**. Change its name, **Questions per exam** or description on the right and click **Save** (or press **Ctrl+S**). The **Active** switch saves as soon as you flip it.

# SMEs
Type part of a name in the box under **SMEs**, pick the person and click **Add**. Anyone in the Personnel file can be picked: adding them gives them the SME role, and access to the Exam Generator, if they don't have it yet. Select an SME and click **Remove** (or right-click them) to take them off this topic; they keep the SME role.

# Questions on hand
The panel at the bottom compares the topic's approved questions with what the overlap rule needs. See **Rules and lists** for how that number is worked out.

# Turning off and deleting
Click **More** (or right-click the topic's row) to turn the topic off or on, or to delete it. A topic can be deleted only while no question, template or exam uses it; otherwise turn it off.

=== templates | Admin: Templates
A template is one kind of exam: the topics it covers, in the order the exam shows them, and how many questions each topic gives.

# Adding and changing a template
Click **New template** (or press **Ctrl+N**) and type its name. Change its name or description on the right and click **Save** (or press **Ctrl+S**). The **Active** switch saves as soon as you flip it; only active templates can be used for a new exam.

# Its topics
- **Add topic** opens a window to pick a topic and set how many questions it gives. The count starts at the topic's own questions per exam, and the window says whether the topic has the approved questions the overlap rule needs for that count.
- **Change** (or double-click a topic) sets the selected topic's count.
- The **arrows** move the selected topic up or down. Exams list their topics in this order.
- **Remove** takes the selected topic off the template. The topic itself stays, and exams already made keep it.

Right-click a topic for the same choices. The line above the topics adds up the topics and questions an exam from this template has.

# Copying and deleting
Click **More** (or right-click the template's row) to copy the template with its topics, or to delete it. A template can be deleted only while no exam has been made from it; otherwise turn it off.

=== people | Admin: People and routing
Who can use the Exam Generator, who holds each role, and so who each step of an exam's approval goes to.

# The approval route
The three cards across the top name who holds **Department head (DH)**, **Executive officer (XO)** and **Senior member (SM)**, the steps an exam goes through after its SMEs sign off. A step nobody holds says so: exams can't pass it until someone does.

# Adding a person
Type part of a name in the box, pick the person and click **Add person**. Only people in the shared Personnel file can be picked. Anyone missing has to be added there first: a Personnel admin can click **Personnel** to do it. Adding someone gives them access to the Exam Generator; give them a role on the right so they have something to open.

# Roles
Click a person in the list, then flip their switches on the right. Each switch saves as soon as you flip it.
- **Can use the Exam Generator**: off stops them signing in to this app.
- **Exam Generator admin**: the Admin pages.
- **Subject matter expert (SME)**: writes questions and reviews the topics they are an SME of (set on the **Topics** page).
- **Department head**, **Executive officer** and **Senior member**: approve questions and exams at their step.

Nobody can turn off their own access or their own admin, and the app always keeps one admin. Taking SME away from someone who is an SME of topics takes them off those topics too, once you agree.

=== rules | Admin: Rules and lists
The overlap rule that limits how far a new exam may repeat recent exams, and the lists questions choose their difficulty and classification from.

# The overlap rule
- **Most of a topic's questions that may repeat**: of the questions a topic gives an exam, at most this share may have been on any of the recent exams. 0% means all must be fresh.
- **Recent exams checked**: how many of the latest exams count as recent.
- **Questions per topic to start with**: what a new topic, and a topic added to a template, starts with.

Moving a slider lists the topics that would be short of approved questions under the new values, before anything is saved. Click **Save** (or press **Ctrl+S**) to use them, or **Undo changes** to go back. Use the **Left** and **Right** arrow keys on a slider to move it a step.

# How many questions a topic needs
If an exam takes n questions from a topic, at least n minus the repeats allowed must be fresh, and those stay recent for the next exams checked. So a topic needs at least (exams checked + 1) x (fresh questions an exam) approved questions to meet the rule exam after exam, using the most questions any active template gives it. For example, 4 questions an exam, 25% may repeat and 10 exams checked: 3 must be fresh, so it needs 11 x 3 = 33.

# Difficulty and classification levels
Click **Add** to add a level. Select a level and click **More**, or right-click it, to rename it, move it, or delete it.
- A difficulty level in use can't be deleted: **turn it off** instead. Questions that have it keep it; new ones can't pick it.
- Classification levels are listed lowest first. An exam's overall marking is the highest level of its questions, so keep the order right. A new level is added as the highest, at the bottom of the list: use **Move up** until it sits above the levels that rank higher. A level in use can't be deleted, but its marking and portion mark can be changed.

=== helptopics | Admin: Help topics
Change the help for any page or dialog, with no new copy of the app. What you save shows at once here, and for everyone else the next time they open the Exam Generator.

# Changing a topic
Click a topic in the list, or type in the search box to find the topics that mention a word. Change its title or text, then click **Save** (or press **Ctrl+S**). **Preview** opens the help window on the topic as it will look. **Undo changes** puts back the saved text.

# Writing the text
- A line starting **#** and a space is a heading.
- A line starting **-** and a space is a bullet.
- Put two stars on each side of a word to make it bold.
- A blank line starts a new paragraph; other lines join into the paragraph above.

# Adding a topic
Click **New topic** (or press **Ctrl+N**). The key ties the topic to what it explains: a page's topic uses the page's key, such as **exams**, and F1 on that page opens it. A topic under another uses its key, a dot and a word, such as **exams.new**, and is listed under it. The key can't change once the topic is saved.

# Where the help comes from
Each new copy of the app brings its own wording for the topics, but only for topics the data file doesn't have yet. Once a topic is in the data file, your wording stays, even when a newer copy is installed.

=== logs | Admin: Logs
The audit trail and the error log, for the admin. Both tabs share the search box and the dates, which start at the last 30 days.

# Audit trail
Every change the app records, newest first: who authored, edited, submitted, approved or sent back a question; who generated, swapped, signed off, approved, sent back or downloaded an exam; who added or changed a reference. **As** is the role the step was taken as. A draft question that was deleted shows as "Question" and its number, marked deleted; its Deleted entry names its key. Nobody can change or delete the audit trail from the app.

# Errors
Every error anyone's copy of the app has hit, newest first, with the page, the procedure and the error. Click an entry to see all of it: the call trail, the form and control, and the computer and build. **Copy** puts the whole entry on the clipboard, to paste into an email to whoever maintains the app.

**Clear log** deletes every entry for every user, after asking. **Export** first to keep a copy.

# Finding entries
- Type in the search box to find names, actions, items, comments or error text. The list changes once you pause typing.
- Change the dates, or clear them to see every date.
- Use a column's funnel to filter by its values.
- A list shows the newest 1,000 entries that fit; the line under the title says when there are more.

**Export** saves the list you see as an Excel or CSV file.

=== settings | My settings
Your notices, your colors and signing out. Each switch saves as soon as you click it, and changes only your own settings.

# Notices
- **Dashboard list**: the reviews, approvals and questions sent back that wait on you, on the Dashboard. On for everyone to start.
- **Emails**: an Outlook email when a review, an approval or a question sent back starts waiting on you. Off to start. The email names the item and the page to open, never a question's text.

Emails go to the address in the Personnel file; the page shows it. Click **Send me a test email** to check that they reach you. Outlook must be set up on this computer, and it may ask you to allow the app to send.

The app sends its emails once your step is saved, from your computer's Outlook. If Outlook was closed, they may wait in your Outbox until you open it.

# Colors
Click **Appearance** to choose the colors the whole app uses on this computer.

# Sign out
Click **Sign out** to let someone else sign in on this copy without closing it. The sign-in screen opens; after the next person signs in, the app opens on their Dashboard with their pages. Closing the sign-in screen closes the app, and so does signing in as someone with no Exam Generator role.
- If you sign in with Windows and have no app password yet, Sign out asks you to choose one first, so only you can sign back in as you. If someone else is signed in on your Windows account and you have no app password, Sign out is refused until you or a Personnel admin sets one.
- Someone signing in on a computer that isn't theirs should leave **Use my Windows sign-in next time** unticked, or this Windows account will sign them in from then on.

=== directory | Personnel
One shared Personnel file holds everyone who can use the Navy apps built on
this kit. Your Windows sign-in finds you in it, so most days you don't sign in
at all. Only Personnel admins can open this page.

# Find someone
- Type part of a name, rank, sign-in name or Windows account in the search box.
- **Can open** says whether they may open this app. **Status** is Admin, Active or Inactive.

# Add people
- **Add person** opens a blank form. It stays open after Save, so you can add the next person straight away.
- **Import** adds everyone in a CSV or Excel file. The first row names the columns: First name and Last name, and any of Middle initial, Rank, Command, Email, Sign-in name and Windows account.
- Imported people can open this app. Rows with no name, or a sign-in name or Windows account someone already has, are skipped and listed.

# Change someone
Double-click them, or pick them and choose **Edit**.

=== directory.person | Add or change a person
- **Windows account** is DOMAIN\user, such as NAVY\jsmith. With it saved, the person is signed in by Windows with no password.
- **Sign-in name** is what they type on the sign-in screen. Leave it blank and it is made from the Windows account, or from the first initial and last name.
- **Active** lets them sign in. Make people inactive rather than deleting them, so their past work still shows their name.
- **Personnel admin** lets them open the Personnel page. There is always at least one active admin, and you can't remove your own admin access.
- **Can open Exam Generator** controls this app only. Each app keeps its own list.

# Passwords
An app password is only needed on the sign-in screen, after Sign out or on a computer where their Windows account isn't saved.
- To reset a forgotten password, type a new one twice and Save, then tell them.
- Passwords need 8 or more characters, and capitals count.

=== directory.signin | Signing in
Exam Generator signs you in with your Windows account when it is saved in Personnel and has access to Exam Generator. This screen shows when it isn't, when it has no access to Exam Generator or is turned off, or after **Sign out** (under **My settings**). It names the Personnel admins to ask.
- Type your sign-in name and app password, then **Sign in**.
- Tick **Use my Windows sign-in next time** to skip this screen on this account.
- No password yet? If this is your own Windows account, you can choose one now. Otherwise ask a Personnel admin to set one.
- **Copy** puts your Windows account on the clipboard, to send to an admin.

=== directory.setup | Setting up Personnel
Exam Generator needs the shared Personnel file and couldn't find it.
- **Find the Personnel file** when another app already uses Personnel. It is usually in a Personnel folder beside the app's data folder.
- **Create a new Personnel file** only for the first app set up here. You become its first admin and add the others.
- **I'm not the admin** shows a note to send your admin, with **Copy**, then closes Exam Generator.

# The site key
Creating the file fills in a new random **site key** that locks it. You can keep it or type your site's own (12 to 20 characters). It is saved as Personnel.key in the same folder, and every app reads it from there, so nobody has to type it again. Keep Personnel.key with the file: without it the file won't open.

# After everyone was deleted
When an admin deleted all data and ticked the Personnel box, the Personnel file is still there but has nobody in it. The next person to open an app sees **Create the first admin**: type your name, rank and app password, and you become the Personnel admin for every app. Then add the others again, or **Import** them. If you are not the admin, choose **I'm not the admin** instead. A backup copy of the old file and its key is in the Backups folder beside it.
